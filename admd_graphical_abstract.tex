\tightheading{Abstract}
Variational quantum eigensolvers can approximate ground states relevant to molecular
and correlated-material modelling, but their accuracy depends strongly on the selected
circuit ansatz. We present a small graph-surrogate benchmark for ranking
particle-number-preserving variational circuits before expensive optimization. The
study includes a four-qubit, two-site spinful Fermi--Hubbard model at six interaction
ratios \(U/t\), together with six-qubit active-space LiH and BeH\(_2\) Hamiltonians
constructed in STO--3G and mapped by Jordan--Wigner. Twelve single- and
double-excitation architectures were evaluated for each Hamiltonian, producing 96 VQE
runs with two deterministic restarts.

Circuit graphs encode excitation type, position, and active spin orbitals, while
Hamiltonian graphs summarize signed Pauli content and interaction-weighted qubit
connectivity. A message-passing representation and validation-selected ridge surrogate
were trained at \(U/t=\{0.5,2,6\}\), tuned only at \(U/t=1\), and tested at the unseen
values \(U/t=\{3,4\}\). The resulting rankings achieved Kendall correlations of 0.606
and 0.667. When only three finalists were fully evaluated, surrogate selection reduced
the mean energy gap relative to random selection by 80\% and 78\%, respectively.
The best LiH and BeH\(_2\) circuits reached gaps of \(7.1\times10^{-5}\) and
\(4.9\times10^{-5}\) hartree. Adding the current Hamiltonian descriptor did not improve
within-family ranking, and zero-shot molecular transfer remained weaker
(\(\bar{\tau}=0.288\)). These results identify graph ranking as promising for
interaction-parameter transfer while motivating learned Hamiltonian encoders for
transferable materials simulation.
